    What assumptions are behind these graphs?

    Each year, 8% of satellites retire and 2% of debris is removed. The model treats objects as equally mixed instead of checking individual orbits. Browser calculations approximate some large counts. The technical methods are described in the project guide.

    UNDER THE HOOD

    A realistic Earth.
    A simplified space sandbox.

    Earth uses photographic surface, night-light, and cloud textures. Satellites follow circular, two-body orbits using Earth’s gravitational parameter. Their speed and period depend on altitude.

    The launch animation is a drawn ascent into the chosen plane. It does not calculate thrust, fuel, drag, stage dynamics, or collision avoidance. Direct launch inclinations must be reachable from the selected latitude without a plane change. Your missions are fictional. The population layer can show selected, timestamped CelesTrak catalog groups with SGP4 propagation; it is not a live tracking feed.

    Earth rotates once per sidereal day. Clouds are a static photographic layer, not live weather. Satellite sizes and spaceport markers are exaggerated for visibility. Your missions stay in this browser.

    From exploration to data science

    The companion Python lab covers Poisson counts, summary statistics, Normal approximations, simulation uncertainty, and PyMC inference. The companion pipeline runs scenario ensembles across Spark workers and stores partitioned Parquet. Experiments shows the results from running the code on one computer. Testing across multiple computers and checking the models against real observations remain future work.
