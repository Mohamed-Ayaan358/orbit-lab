    UNDER THE HOOD

    A realistic Earth.
    A simplified space sandbox.

    Earth uses photographic surface, night-light, and cloud textures. Satellites follow circular, two-body orbits using Earth’s gravitational parameter. Their speed and period depend on altitude.

    The launch animation is a drawn ascent into the chosen plane. It does not simulate thrust, fuel, drag, staging, or collision avoidance. Direct launch inclinations must be reachable from the selected latitude without a plane change. Satellites here are fictional; there is no live tracking feed.

    Earth rotates once per sidereal day. Clouds are a static photographic layer, not live weather. Satellite sizes and spaceport markers are exaggerated for visibility. Your missions stay in this browser.

    From exploration to data science

    The companion Python lab covers Poisson counts, summary statistics, Normal approximations, simulation uncertainty, and PyMC inference. A larger project could run scenario ensembles across Spark workers and store the results as partitioned Parquet. This website alone is not a distributed big data system.
