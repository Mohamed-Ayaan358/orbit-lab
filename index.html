    THE DATA BEHIND THE VIEW

    Explore the possible futures.

    Synthetic teaching scenarios · not a calibrated debris forecast.

    Probability graphs are hypothetical teaching-model exposure estimates. They are not calculated from catalog conjunctions. All custom orbits share the same model rate; altitude, inclination and visible marker size do not change it.

    Retirement: 8% / year. Debris removal: 2% / year. Homogeneous mixing. Browser large-count sampling is approximate; Python uses exact Poisson, Binomial and Negative Binomial sampling. Lognormal sums use moment matching in both engines.

    UNDER THE HOOD

    A realistic Earth.
    A simplified space sandbox.

    Earth uses photographic surface, night-light, and cloud textures. Satellites follow circular, two-body orbits using Earth’s gravitational parameter. Their speed and period depend on altitude.

    The launch animation is a drawn ascent into the chosen plane. It does not calculate thrust, fuel, drag, stage dynamics, or collision avoidance. Direct launch inclinations must be reachable from the selected latitude without a plane change. Your missions are fictional. The population layer can show selected, timestamped CelesTrak catalog groups with SGP4 propagation; it is not a live tracking feed.

    Earth rotates once per sidereal day. Clouds are a static photographic layer, not live weather. Satellite sizes and spaceport markers are exaggerated for visibility. Your missions stay in this browser.

    From exploration to data science

    The companion Python lab covers Poisson counts, summary statistics, Normal approximations, simulation uncertainty, and PyMC inference. The companion pipeline runs scenario ensembles across Spark workers and stores partitioned Parquet. The Science section shows a local execution benchmark.
